Paintings that let students see the cells biology class talks about

Science You Can See is a student art exhibit of sickle cells, cancer cells, the stages of mitosis and the path from gene to protein. Each painting comes with a short title card that explains what is on the canvas.

A painter who has also worked in a cancer research lab

I'm Lily Butler, a senior at Episcopal Collegiate School in Little Rock, and I paint the parts of biology that are hardest to imagine.

I won Best in Class in Biology as a freshman and in Genetics as a junior, and one of my paintings was shown at the Arkansas Museum of Fine Arts in ninth grade. This project is where those two halves meet: I paint the cells and processes that are hardest to imagine, so more students can see that science is within their reach.

This past summer I spent six weeks as a Stephens Summer Science Scholar at the University of Arkansas for Medical Sciences, researching metastatic melanoma and ER stress in a working lab, and presented my research at the UAMS undergraduate research symposium.

How to learn a cell by looking at it first

Three steps turn a vocabulary list into a picture you can remember.

  1. Look at the painting

    Start with the shapes and colors. Notice what looks crowded, bent, split or stretched before you read a single word.

  2. Read the title card

    Every painting has a short card that names the cell or stage and explains what is happening in plain language.

  3. Take the picture back to class

    Next time your biology teacher brings up mitosis, red blood cells or protein synthesis, you will already have an image to hang the terms on.

What changes when students can see the science

When cells stay invisible

  • Biology turns into a list of words to memorize the night before a test.
  • Students decide they are not science people and stop signing up for science classes.
  • Careers in medicine and research start to feel out of reach.

When you can picture them

  • You remember what happens in each stage of mitosis because you have seen it.
  • You walk into biology class with an image for every term.
  • You start to see science as something you can understand and enjoy.
